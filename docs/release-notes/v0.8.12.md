# v0.8.12 — Round Clock recovers personal rounds and cooldowns across world-time jumps

A character could finish a spell, travel for hours and arrive still waiting to cast again.
During travel, rest and scripted jumps, the engine can advance the world clock in one step.
Several personal timers usually advance only when the engine updates that creature. Round Clock
(component 361) counts the elapsed ticks toward those timers, including when the world-screen
hour advance is used. Recovery follows each creature's current update speed and applies to
locally owned party members and creatures in loaded areas.

- **Spells and items.** Time spent travelling can clear the aura used by a completed spell or
  item use, so the character can cast again or use another item on arrival.
- **Attacks.** The unused part of an attack round can expire after a completed swing.
  The next attack still uses the engine's normal attack and damage rolls.
- **Hide in Shadows.** Time counts toward the lockout after a failed or broken Hide. When the
  thief is ready again, the stock Stealth button enables on its first paused frame and accepts
  a click before another AI tick. The engine still rolls the Hide attempt.
- **Contingencies.** Elapsed time can make the next check due. The engine evaluates the
  contingency's conditions on its next eligible poll.

A zero-tick area or door transition grants no free action because no world time elapsed. The
same applies when a rest is interrupted before the clock advances. Loading a save restores a
clock reading and grants no recovery. The engine retains control of active casts, attacks,
item transactions and the script timer set by `MakeUnselectable`. During Time Stop only the
caster and creatures immune to the stop can recover.

## Installation

On Windows x64 **2.7.3.0**, component 361 supports BG:EE, SoD, BG2:EE, EET and IWD:EE with
EEex v1.2.0 or v1.3.1, using embedded Lua 5.2 or LuaJIT. Install EEex first. Select
`cbr_time_jump_round_cooldowns` (361) in the full installer, or extract
`CBR_ROUND_CLOCK-v0.8.12-windows.zip` into the game folder and run `Setup-cbr_round_clock.exe`.
Both installers supply the same runtime. Choose one for 361. True Time Stop (360) is independent.
Start the game through InfinityLoader and look for `[CBR Round Clock] on` in the loader log.

## Verification

- **Offline.** Full discovery ran 1,015 tests. **986 passed, 29 skipped, zero failures or
  errors.** The skips cover three unstaged executable images and optional dragon, Flail of
  the Ages and bracers captures. The Win64 check walked captured call frames with
  `RtlLookupFunctionEntry` and `RtlVirtualUnwind`. Earlier checks walked frames generated
  by both EEex release writers under Lua 5.2 and LuaJIT. A BG2:EE process also walked the
  registered frames at the Lua callback and both toolbar calls.
- **Final BG2:EE smoke.** Components 360 and 361 ran together at `70c9e06` on 2.7.3.0 with
  EEex v1.3.1 and LuaJIT. On paused frame 388, `DayNight(13)` advanced the clock by 26,990
  ticks. All four seeded timers recovered. The stock Stealth button enabled on its first
  paint and its click entered Stealth mode on that same frame.
- **Earlier single-player quiet labs.** BG2:EE world-map trips of 90,000 ticks and a completed
  rest of 36,000 ticks recovered all four timers. A first-attempt rest interruption advanced
  zero ticks and preserved all four. Across 12 scripted spell and potion trials on BG2:EE
  and standalone IWD:EE with LuaJIT, the next spell started one tick after resume when queued
  after native area compression finished. IWD:EE also accepted the stock paused Stealth click
  with both LuaJIT and embedded Lua 5.2. These trials used EEex v1.3.1.

Waylaid travel, custom UI action bars, a live multiplayer session and a full BG:EE/SoD runtime
launch remain open. Multiplayer forced clock synchronization has static verification only.
